Copyright Registration Guide for Final-Year IT 
Engineering Projects 
India • Student/Faculty Reference • Updated August 2026 
(Important: Academic preparation guide, not legal advice. The current Copyright Office portal, Copyright Act/Rules 
and institutional IPR policy control if there is any discrepancy.) 
 
 
1. Purpose 
This guide helps final-year IT engineering students identify copyrightable project outputs and prepare a practical 
document package for online copyright registration in India. 
 
Computer programmes are a recognized class of copyright work. 
 
A copyright application is for one work at a time. 
 
For software, copyright concerns original expression/code; it does not turn an abstract idea or algorithm into 
copyright. 
 
Confirm who owns the work before filing—student, joint authors, institution or sponsor.

4. What Students Must Not Claim 
 
Open-source libraries/frameworks as their own. 
 
GitHub code without checking its licence and rights. 
 
Third-party datasets, images, icons, fonts, music, videos, APIs or pretrained models merely because they are online. 
 
The abstract project idea/problem statement as software expression. 
 
Patent rights merely by registering copyright. 
5. Official Online Filing — High-Level Workflow 
 
Create/login to the official Copyright Office portal. 
 
Select online Copyright Registration. 
 
Complete Form XIV and save. 
 
Keep signatures, work and supporting documents ready for upload. 
 
Upload the work and required documents. 
 
Complete Statement of Further Particulars where applicable. 
 
Pay the prescribed fee through the portal. 
 
Record the Diary Number and retain the submitted package. 
 
Monitor status/deficiencies and respond as required. 
Important: The official online instructions currently state that uploaded documents should not be password protected. 
They specify PDF/JPG for artistic works, MP3 for sound recordings, and PDF for literary/dramatic, musical and 
software works. For software, the portal currently specifies a PDF containing at least the first 10 and last 10 pages 
of source code, or the entire source code if it is fewer than 20 pages, with no blocked-out/redacted portions.

7. Preparing the Source-Code PDF 
 
Use the exact software title/version corresponding to the registered work. 
 
Include a simple cover page identifying title, version/date, applicant/owner and authors. 
 
Follow the current upload rule: first 10 and last 10 pages, or entire code if fewer than 20 pages. 
 
Do not black out/redact required source-code pages where the portal prohibits it. 
 
Use readable formatting and page numbers. 
 
Do not upload passwords, API keys, private keys, credentials or sensitive personal data. Keep a secure internal 
evidence copy. 
 
Retain Git/version-control history and dated development records. 
Suggested cover page 
TITLE: <Exact Software Title> 
VERSION/DATE: <Version and date> 
CLASS: Computer Software / Computer Programme 
APPLICANT/OWNER: <Name> 
AUTHORS: <Names> 
LANGUAGE(S): <Python/Java/etc.> 
PROJECT ID: <Institution ID> 
 
8. Authorship vs Ownership 
Author and copyright owner can be different. Form XIV and the Statement of Particulars ask for the applicant's 
interest/ownership information. Confirm the institution's IPR policy, guide/sponsor agreement and any assignment/NOC 
requirements before filing.

12. Statement of Further Particulars 
The official online instructions state that the Statement of Further Particulars applies to Literary/Dramatic, Musical, 
Artistic and Software works. Students should use the fields displayed by the current portal rather than relying on old 
screenshots or third-party forms. 
13. Open-Source and Third-Party Material Register 
Open-source software remains copyrighted software distributed under licence. Students may be able to use, modify and 
distribute it under that licence, but cannot claim the upstream work as their own.

14. Patent Timing — Important 
If the project contains a potentially patentable technical invention, discuss patent filing before public disclosure. A 
copyright registration does not substitute for patent protection. A paper, poster, public demonstration, website or 
repository can create patentability issues depending on the facts.

15. Final-Year Project Copyright Checklist 
 
☐ Final project title is consistent everywhere. 
 
☐ Applicant/owner is confirmed under institutional policy. 
 
☐ All authors/contributors are accurately identified. 
 
☐ Original code is separated/documented from third-party code. 
 
☐ Open-source licenses have been checked. 
 
☐ Dataset/images/fonts/models/APIs have been checked. 
 
☐ Source-code PDF follows current Copyright Office upload instructions. 
 
☐ No prohibited redactions/blocked source-code portions are present. 
 
☐ No passwords/API keys/private keys/sensitive credentials are included. 
 
☐ NOCs/assignments/agreements are ready where applicable. 
 
☐ Form XIV/Statement of Particulars/Statement of Further Particulars information is checked. 
 
☐ Uploaded files are not password protected. 
 
☐ Patent/IPR cell has been consulted if patentability may exist. 
 
☐ Copies of submission, receipt, fee proof and Diary Number are retained.

18. Source-Based Notes for Students 
The official Copyright Office states that Form XIV is the prescribed application; the Rules require an application to be 
for one work and specify source-code requirements for computer-programme registration. The online filing instructions 
provide the current upload formats and software source-code-page instructions. Check these official resources 
immediately before submission because portal procedures and technical upload requirements can change.
