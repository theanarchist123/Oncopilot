ABSTRACT 
Breast cancer is among the most prevalent malignancies affecting women worldwide and 
represents a significant healthcare burden. Effective treatment planning requires oncologists to 
analyze multiple clinical parameters, including tumor characteristics, molecular biomarkers, 
staging information, lymph node involvement, and patient comorbidities. However, these 
critical data points are often distributed across multiple diagnostic documents such as 
histopathology reports, immunohistochemistry (IHC) panels, radiology findings, and 
laboratory tests. This fragmentation of clinical information increases the complexity of 
decision-making and places a substantial cognitive burden on healthcare professionals. 
As oncology treatment pathways become increasingly complex and personalized, there is a 
growing need for intelligent systems that can consolidate patient data and support clinicians in 
aligning treatment decisions with established medical guidelines. Clinical Decision Support 
Systems (CDSS) have been developed to address this need by analyzing patient information and 
generating evidence-based recommendations. However, many existing CDSS platforms face 
key limitations, including limited transparency, high implementation costs, and reliance on 
opaque machine learning models that do not provide clear reasoning for their outputs. The lack 
of explainability in such systems reduces clinician trust and limits their adoption in real-world 
clinical settings. 
To address these challenges, this project proposes an AI-Assisted Clinical Decision Support 
System for Breast Cancer Treatment Planning that emphasizes explainable and 
guideline-driven decision making. The system integrates Natural Language Processing 
(NLP) techniques with a transparent rule-based clinical reasoning engine derived from 
internationally recognized oncology guidelines, including NCCN, ESMO, ASCO, and ISMPO. 
It automatically extracts clinically relevant information from diagnostic reports, transforms it 
into structured patient profiles, and evaluates the data using explicit IF–THEN clinical rules. 
Each recommendation is accompanied by a clear explanation linking the decision to specific 
clinical parameters and guideline references, ensuring full traceability and interpretability. 
To further enhance safety and reliability, the system incorporates a clinical data validation 
module that flags missing or uncertain information for clinician review before generating 
recommendations. In addition, a contraindication detection module evaluates potential 
treatment risks based on patient comorbidities and therapy-specific toxicity considerations. 
The final treatment pathways are presented through a doctor dashboard, where clinicians can 
review the structured data, inspect the reasoning behind each recommendation, and modify or 
override system outputs, thereby maintaining full clinical control. 
Given the sensitive nature of healthcare data, the system is designed with strong security and 
privacy safeguards, including encrypted data storage and transmission, role-based access control 
(RBAC), audit logging, and privacy-by-design principles aligned with HIPAA standards. By 
combining automated data extraction, explainable clinical reasoning, and secure system 
architecture, the proposed solution aims to improve the efficiency, transparency, and reliability of 
breast cancer treatment planning while reinforcing clinician trust and preserving human oversight 
in the decision-making process.

INTRODUCTION 
Breast cancer represents one of the most significant public health challenges globally and is the 
most common cancer among women in India, accounting for approximately 27–30% of all 
female cancers. Recent estimates indicate that 1 in 28 women in India is likely to develop 
breast cancer during her lifetime, with higher incidence observed in urban populations. This 
rising burden underscores the need for efficient, accurate, and scalable approaches to treatment 
planning within the Indian healthcare context. 
Advances in oncology research have established that breast cancer is not a single disease but a 
heterogeneous group of malignancies characterized by diverse molecular and biological 
profiles. These variations significantly influence tumor behavior, treatment response, and 
patient prognosis, making precision medicine central to modern breast cancer management. 
Treatment strategies are therefore increasingly tailored based on the unique clinical and 
molecular characteristics of each patient. 
A critical component of this personalized approach is the analysis of molecular biomarkers, 
typically obtained through immunohistochemistry (IHC) testing. The most clinically relevant 
biomarkers include Estrogen Receptor (ER), Progesterone Receptor (PR), Human 
Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2), and the Ki-67 proliferation index. ER and PR 
determine hormone sensitivity, HER2 indicates tumor aggressiveness and eligibility for 
targeted therapy, and Ki-67 reflects tumor proliferation rate. While additional biomarkers 
such as p53 mutations, BRCA1/2 genetic markers, and genomic assay scores (e.g., Oncotype 
DX) are used in advanced settings, this project focuses on ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67 due to 
their widespread availability, standardization, and routine use in Indian clinical practice. 
This ensures that the proposed system remains practical and applicable in real-world 
healthcare settings, particularly in resource-constrained environments. 
These biomarkers are used to classify tumors into molecular subtypes such as Luminal A, 
Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC), each associated 
with distinct biological characteristics and treatment pathways. In parallel, oncologists evaluate 
the Tumor-Node-Metastasis (TNM) staging system, which incorporates tumor size, lymph 
node involvement, and distant metastasis. Among these, lymph node status is a critical 
prognostic factor, as it indicates whether cancer has spread beyond the primary tumor. The 
number and extent of affected lymph nodes directly influence disease stage, recurrence risk, 
and treatment decisions, including the need for chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and 
surgical intervention such as axillary lymph node dissection. 
Despite advances in diagnostic technologies, a major challenge in oncology practice remains the 
fragmentation of clinical data. Patient information relevant to treatment planning is often 
distributed across multiple reports generated by different diagnostic departments. Pathology 
reports, imaging studies, biomarker test results, and laboratory findings are frequently 
stored in separate systems, requiring clinicians to manually review and synthesize information 
before making treatment decisions. This process is time-consuming and increases the risk of 
overlooking critical clinical details. 
To address these challenges, Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS) have been developed 
to assist clinicians in analyzing patient data and recommending guideline-based treatment 
options. CDSS platforms compare patient information against a structured knowledge base of 
clinical guidelines and decision rules, enabling more efficient and consistent treatment planning. 
4 
 
However, many existing oncology CDSS platforms rely heavily on black-box machine learning 
models, which lack transparency and explainability, thereby reducing clinician trust. 
Additionally, such systems often require significant infrastructure and financial investment, 
limiting their adoption in many Indian healthcare settings. 
The AI-Assisted Clinical Decision Support System proposed in this project addresses these 
limitations by emphasizing explainability, transparency, and accessibility within the Indian 
clinical context. Instead of relying on opaque machine learning models, the system employs a 
rule-based decision engine derived from established oncology guidelines, ensuring that every 
recommendation is traceable to specific clinical parameters and guideline logic. Furthermore, 
the system integrates Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques to extract structured 
clinical information from unstructured diagnostic reports, reducing manual effort and improving 
efficiency. 
This project specifically focuses on the Indian demographic, where variations in genetic 
factors, healthcare access, diagnostic practices, and treatment protocols influence clinical 
decision-making. Consequently, the system is designed using guideline adaptations and clinical 
practices relevant to India, and its outputs are not intended to be directly generalized to other 
populations without appropriate validation. By integrating automated data extraction, 
explainable reasoning, clinician oversight, and secure data management, the proposed 
system aims to support oncologists in delivering efficient, accurate, and patient-centered 
breast cancer care.

PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Breast cancer treatment planning is a complex clinical process that requires the integration of 
multiple diagnostic parameters derived from diverse sources of medical information. Oncologists 
must analyze pathology reports, immunohistochemistry results, radiology findings, staging 
information, lymph node status, and patient health history before determining an appropriate 
treatment strategy. The increasing complexity of modern oncology diagnostics has made this 
decision-making process highly data-intensive and cognitively demanding. 
A key challenge in breast cancer management is the fragmentation of patient data across 
multiple diagnostic documents. Clinical information such as tumor characteristics, 
biomarker status (ER, PR, HER2, Ki-67), lymph node involvement, and staging details is 
often distributed across separate reports generated by different departments. Clinicians are 
required to manually consolidate this information to construct a comprehensive clinical profile, 
which can be both time-consuming and error-prone. 
The manual interpretation of clinical reports introduces the risk of overlooking critical 
indicators, particularly in high-volume healthcare settings. For instance, misinterpretation or 
omission of HER2 status, Ki-67 index, or lymph node involvement can significantly impact 
treatment decisions. Lymph node involvement, in particular, is a crucial determinant of disease 
progression and treatment intensity, as it directly influences cancer staging, recurrence risk, 
and the need for systemic therapies such as chemotherapy and radiation. 
Another significant challenge is the rapid evolution of clinical oncology guidelines. 
Organizations such as NCCN, ESMO, and ASCO frequently update their treatment 
recommendations based on emerging clinical evidence. Keeping up with these updates requires 
continuous effort from clinicians, particularly in resource-constrained and high-patient-load 
environments such as those commonly found in India.Additionally patients often face difficulty 
understanding their diagnosis and treatment plans. Breast cancer treatment typically involves 
multiple stages, including surgery, che​
motherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, 
and endocrine therapy. The use of complex medical terminology in clinical reports can lead to 
confusion, anxiety, and reduced patient engagement. 
Furthermore, the security and privacy of patient data is a critical concern. Healthcare systems 
must ensure that sensitive medical information is protected against unauthorized access, data 
breaches, and misuse. Any system designed to support clinical decision-making must therefore 
incorporate robust security mechanisms, including encryption, authentication, and 
role-based access control.Despite the availability of CDSS tools, there remains a lack of 
accessible, explainable, and context-aware systems tailored to the Indian healthcare 
environment. Existing systems often lack transparency, integration with clinical workflows, 
and adaptability to region-specific practices, limiting their effectiveness in real-world 
deployment. 
Therefore, the problem addressed in this project is the absence of a transparent, explainable, 
and secure clinical decision support system capable of integrating fragmented diagnostic data, 
incorporating biomarker-driven and lymph node–based clinical reasoning, and supporting 
oncologists in planning breast cancer treatment pathways. The proposed solution aims to address 
this gap by combining automated data extraction, explainable rule-based reasoning, clinical 
validation mechanisms, and strong data privacy protections within a unified platform 
designed for practical use in the Indian healthcare context.

PROPOSED SOLUTION  
The proposed solution is an AI-Assisted Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) designed to 
support oncologists in planning treatment pathways for breast cancer patients. The system 
focuses on converting unstructured clinical reports into structured medical data and applying 
guideline-based reasoning to generate treatment recommendations that are transparent, 
explainable, and clinically safe. 
At the core of the system is an automated data extraction pipeline powered by Natural 
Language Processing (NLP). Diagnostic reports such as pathology reports, other test results, 
and staging summaries are uploaded into the system. These documents often contain large 
volumes of unstructured text describing tumor characteristics, receptor status, and other 
diagnostic findings. The NLP pipeline processes these reports to identify and extract key clinical 
indicators such as tumor size, lymph node involvement, ER status, PR status, HER2 
expression, Ki-67 proliferation index, and disease stage. 
The extracted information is converted into a structured patient profile, which represents the 
patient’s oncological characteristics in a standardized format. Structured data enables the system 
to perform computational reasoning and evaluate treatment rules efficiently. 
To ensure clinical safety, the system incorporates a Clinical Data Validation Module. This 
module verifies whether essential fields required for treatment planning are present. For example, 
biomarker information such as HER2 status or lymph node count is critical for determining 
treatment pathways. If any required field is missing or ambiguous, the system automatically 
generates a flag for clinician review. This ensures that incomplete or uncertain information does 
not lead to incorrect treatment recommendations. 
After the patient data has been validated, the system performs molecular subtype classification. 
Breast cancer is commonly classified into molecular subtypes based on receptor status and 
proliferation markers. These subtypes include Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and 
Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC). Each subtype has distinct treatment strategies and 
prognosis. The system applies classification rules derived from established oncology guidelines 
to determine the molecular subtype. 
Once subtype classification is complete, the patient profile is evaluated by the Rule-Based 
Decision Engine. This engine contains a structured knowledge base of treatment rules derived 
from internationally recognized oncology guidelines, including NCCN, ESMO, ASCO, and 
ISMPO clinical practice guidelines. Each rule represents a specific clinical condition and 
associated treatment pathway. 
For example, a simplified rule may be represented as follows: 
If ER positive and HER2 negative with low Ki-67, classify as Luminal A and recommend 
endocrine therapy. 
Similarly, treatment pathways may depend on lymph node involvement. For instance, Luminal 
A tumors that are node-negative may be treated primarily with endocrine therapy, whereas 
node-positive Luminal A tumors may require additional chemotherapy depending on 
clinical risk factors. 
 
7 
 
In addition to identifying treatment pathways, the system performs Contraindication and Safety 
Analysis. This module evaluates potential treatment risks based on patient health conditions. 
Certain chemotherapy drugs may pose risks for patients with cardiac disease or kidney 
dysfunction. If a treatment recommendation conflicts with the patient’s medical condition, the 
system generates a safety warning. These warnings alert clinicians to potential complications 
and encourage careful evaluation before finalizing treatment plans. 
Once treatment pathways have been identified and safety checks completed, the system presents 
the results through the Doctor Dashboard. This interface displays a comprehensive patient 
summary, including biomarker status, disease stage, and recommended treatment options. Each 
recommendation is accompanied by an explanation showing which clinical rules were triggered 
and which guideline supports the recommendation. 
The system follows a Human-in-the-Loop Design Principle, meaning that the final treatment 
decision remains under the authority of the clinician. Doctors can review, modify, or override the 
system’s recommendations. Any modifications made by clinicians are recorded in the system’s 
audit log for transparency and accountability. 
Given the sensitivity of medical information, the system integrates robust security and privacy 
mechanisms. These include data encryption, secure authentication, role-based access control 
(RBAC), and audit logging. The design follows privacy-by-design principles inspired by 
HIPAA data protection standards, ensuring that patient information remains secure throughout 
the system lifecycle. 
By combining automated data extraction, guideline-based reasoning, clinical validation, and 
secure architecture, the proposed solution aims to support oncologists in delivering accurate, 
efficient, and patient-centered breast cancer treatment planning.

METHODOLOGY/BLOCK 
DIAGRAM  
The proposed system follows a structured methodology consisting of multiple interconnected 
modules that process clinical data and generate treatment recommendations. 
The workflow begins with the Data Ingestion Phase, where clinicians upload diagnostic reports 
into the system. These reports may include pathology reports, test results, radiology 
summaries, or laboratory reports. The system accepts multiple document formats, including 
PDF, text files, and structured electronic health records. 
Once uploaded, the reports undergo Preprocessing. In this stage, the system converts document 
contents into machine-readable text using document parsing and optical character recognition if 
necessary. The text is then prepared for analysis through tokenization, normalization, and 
sentence segmentation. 
Following preprocessing, the system executes the Natural Language Processing (NLP) 
Pipeline. This module uses a combination of Named Entity Recognition (NER) models and 
rule-based pattern matching to identify key clinical entities. Important terms such as ER status, 
HER2 status, tumor size, lymph node involvement, and disease stage are extracted from the 
report text. 
After data extraction, the system performs Clinical Data Validation. This step verifies whether 
all required clinical indicators are available. If essential fields are missing, the system generates a 
clinical validation alert and requests manual confirmation from the clinician. 
Validated data is then processed by the Subtype Classification Module. Using biomarker-based 
rules, the module determines the molecular subtype of the tumor. The subtype classification 
result forms the basis for selecting appropriate treatment pathways. 
Next, the structured patient profile is evaluated by the Rule-Based Decision Engine. The engine 
compares the patient data against a set of guideline-derived decision rules. These rules 
represent treatment recommendations established by oncology organizations. Each rule consists 
of a clinical condition and an associated treatment recommendation. 
The output of the decision engine is passed to the Contraindication and Safety Module, which 
analyzes potential treatment risks. If a treatment recommendation conflicts with patient 
comorbidities or laboratory values, the system generates a safety alert for clinician review. 
Finally, the treatment recommendations are presented through the Doctor Dashboard Interface. 
Clinicians review the recommendations and either approve or modify the suggested treatment 
plan.

personalized cancer treatment planning. The process begins with the upload of clinical

The system architecture provides a comprehensive workflow for processing clinical data and 
generating personalized cancer treatment recommendations. It begins with the user interaction 
layer, where the oncologist uploads patient reports and the patient can access the treatment

The uploaded clinical documents, including pathology reports, biomarker reports, imaging 
results, and patient health information, are collected in the clinical document upload module. 
These documents are then processed through a document processing pipeline, which involves file

Next, the system performs NLP-based clinical data extraction to identify key medical features 
such as tumor stage (TNM), receptor status (ER, PR, HER2), Ki-67 index, tumor size, lymph

structured patient case profile. 
The patient profile is then evaluated using a guideline-based treatment evaluation module, which 
follows established clinical protocols to determine suitable treatment strategies. Based on this 
evaluation, the system generates treatment pathway suggestions such as endocrine therapy, 
HER2-targeted therapy, chemotherapy, combination therapy, and immunotherapy for specific 
cases like TNBC.

ensure that the suggested treatments are compatible with the patient’s condition. This includes 
checks for cardiac and renal function, drug contraindications, and allergies. 
The final recommendations are reviewed by the doctor in the clinical review module, where the 
doctor can validate, modify, and finalize the treatment plan. The approved plan is then displayed 
through the output dashboard. 
Additionally, the system includes a feedback and outcome tracking mechanism, where doctor 
approvals or overrides and treatment outcomes are recorded. This data is used to update the 
model and knowledge base, creating a continuous improvement loop that enhances the system’s 
performance over time.

HARDWARE AND SOFTWARE REQUIRED  
The development and deployment of the proposed system require both hardware infrastructure 
and specialized software components. 
Hardware Requirements 
The system can be developed and tested on a standard personal computer or workstation. 
Recommended specifications include a multi-core processor, a minimum of 8 GB RAM, and 
sufficient storage capacity for document processing and database operations. Internet 
connectivity is required for cloud deployment and collaboration. 
Software Requirements 
The frontend interface is implemented using Next.js, a modern web development framework 
based on React. Next.js supports server-side rendering and provides efficient performance for 
interactive web applications. 
The backend server is implemented using FastAPI, a high-performance Python framework 
designed for building scalable web APIs. FastAPI provides automatic API documentation and 
supports asynchronous processing, which is beneficial for handling multiple concurrent requests. 
The system uses PostgreSQL as the primary database management system. PostgreSQL 
provides strong reliability, advanced query capabilities, and support for structured data storage. 
The Natural Language Processing module is implemented using Python libraries such as 
spaCy, combined with rule-based extraction methods for clinical terminology. This hybrid 
approach helps reduce NLP errors while maintaining high extraction accuracy. 
Development tools include Visual Studio Code for programming, GitHub for version 
control, and Docker for containerized deployment. Containerization ensures consistent system 
behavior across different environments. 
Security Tools 
Security features are implemented using TLS encryption for secure data transmission, 
AES-256 encryption for stored data, and JWT-based authentication mechanisms. 
Role-based access control ensures that users can only access information relevant to their role.

PROPOSED EVALUATION MEASURES  
The effectiveness of the system will be evaluated through multiple evaluation criteria covering 
technical performance, clinical accuracy, usability, and security. 
NLP Extraction Accuracy 
The Natural Language Processing module will be evaluated using standard metrics including 
precision, recall, and F1-score. These metrics measure the system’s ability to correctly extract 
clinical entities from diagnostic reports. 
Clinical Decision Accuracy 
The rule-based decision engine will be validated using a set of simulated breast cancer case 
scenarios. System recommendations will be compared against treatment guidelines provided by 
oncology organizations. The level of agreement between system outputs and guideline 
recommendations will be measured. 
System Usability 
Usability will be evaluated using the System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire. Clinicians 
will interact with the doctor dashboard and provide feedback on ease of use, interface design, and 
workflow integration. 
Security and Privacy Testing 
Security evaluation will verify the implementation of encryption protocols, authentication 
mechanisms, and role-based access control. The system will be tested for vulnerabilities such as 
unauthorized data access or insecure communication channels. 
System Performance and Clinical Validation 
System performance will be evaluated based on response time, throughput, and reliability 
under simulated clinical workloads to ensure the platform remains responsive during real-world 
usage. 
In addition to technical performance, clinical decision accuracy will be assessed through 
validation by practicing oncologists. System-generated treatment recommendations will be 
compared with doctor opinions and guideline-based decisions to measure correctness and 
clinical relevance. 
The system will also incorporate a doctor feedback mechanism, where clinicians can review, 
accept, modify, or reject recommendations. This feedback will be recorded and used to evaluate 
system effectiveness and identify areas for improvement. 
Over time, the system can evolve using a learning-based improvement approach, where 
feedback from previous cases and outcomes of subsequent patient analyses are used to refine 
decision rules. This creates a reinforcement learning-inspired feedback loop, enabling 
continuous enhancement of system performance while maintaining clinician oversight.

CONCLUSION  
The proposed system demonstrates a structured and scalable approach to handling complex 
clinical data derived from oncology guidelines such as NCCN and ESMO. Unstructured medical 
documents are effectively processed using Natural Language Processing techniques, enabling the 
extraction of key clinical entities including biomarkers (ER, HER2, Ki-67), nodal status, 
comorbidities, and treatment indicators. Data preprocessing steps such as text cleaning, 
normalization, and entity mapping ensure that noisy and fragmented clinical text is transformed 
into a consistent and machine-readable format. 
The algorithmic framework combines rule-based reasoning with contextual understanding 
through ClinicalBERT embeddings, allowing the system to capture both explicit guideline logic 
and implicit semantic relationships within clinical text. A priority-based decision engine ensures 
clinically relevant and conflict-free recommendations by considering multiple patient attributes 
simultaneously. Furthermore, the integration of comorbidity and contraindication handling 
enhances the safety and real-world applicability of the system. 
An important strength of the proposed approach lies in its adaptability. The feedback-driven 
learning mechanism enables continuous refinement of rules based on clinician input, thereby 
improving recommendation accuracy over time. This hybrid architecture combining deterministic 
rules, machine learning representations, and human-in-the-loop feedback provides a robust 
foundation for building explainable and clinically assistive decision support systems. 
Overall, the system effectively bridges the gap between unstructured medical knowledge and 
actionable clinical insights, offering a reliable, interpretable, and extensible solution for 
AI-assisted healthcare decision-making.
